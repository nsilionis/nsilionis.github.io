Currently a postdoctoral researcher working on structural health monitoring of ship structures (mainly). Primarily interested in the intersection of computational mechanics and probabilistic modelling towards imroving decision-making in the operation and management of engineering structures (and more...).

Current research focuses on quantifying the value of SHM for structural maintenance planning of ships to strengthen the case for industrial deployment. Applications include quantifying the VoI from SHM systems monitoring thickness loss and enhancing the robustness of monitoring systems under data loss using adaptive generative models.

Past works include using Bayesian decision theory to assess the utility of different physics-based models used for structural derioration monitoring, determining optimal sensor configurations for strain-based thickness loss estimation, and using deep generative models for efficient full-field structural response reconstruction.

Research interests span structural health monitoring (SHM), Bayesian inference & uncertainty quantification, machine learning for structural analysis, ship hull structural reliability
